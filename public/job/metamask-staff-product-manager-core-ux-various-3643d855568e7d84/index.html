MetaMask: Staff Product Manager, Core UX

🏢 Various 📍 Remote 💼 Full-time 💰 Not disclosed

Job Description

Headquarters: United States - Remote, EMEA - Remote Please note that we are unable to consider applications from candidates based in France, Italy, or Germany for this role. Money is moving onto the internet, and the shift has a name: Open Money. This is money that is open, portable, agentic, and owned by you. MetaMask spent the last ten years building it; with 100M+ downloads, users in ~190 countries, and trillions in cumulative transaction volume, it's the most trusted self-custodial financial platform on the internet. Now we're building the operating system for your money: one place to hold, move, grow, and use anything you own. Join a remote-first, global team rebuilding how money works: a problem that touches everyone, every day. About MetaMask & Trade MetaMask is a self-custodial wallet used by tens of millions of people. In Trade, people swap, bridge, trade perps, predict and earn. Core UX is the part of MetaMask mobile that sit inside: Home, the navigation, balances, tokens and positions, and the path from the first screen into each of them. People open the app and land on Home. If that screen is slow, crowded, or unclear about what to do next, Swap, Perps, Predict and Earn see fewer of the people who opened the app. The surface is live. You start from the app people already have, and you keep shipping on it. The role is Staff Product Manager, Core UX. You write the vision for Home and the navigation, and you run the build with design and engineering. A design background is a requirement, because the work is the screen itself. The Role You are the product manager for Core UX on MetaMask mobile. You own the vision, the roadmap and the backlog for Home and the navigation, and you own whether that screen is working. You will work closely with: The Core UX engineering team Design Data and analytics The product managers for Swap, Perps, Predict and Earn Those products show up on Home. You own how a person reaches them from there, and how their balances and positions show up. Their roadmaps stay with their product managers. Yours is Home and the navigation. The app is already live and people open it every day, so you start from what is there. You walk design leadership through the screen, and you are in the reviews where the layout gets decided. What You'll Do Set the Core UX vision Write what Home is for, who has to succeed on it, and what it shows before anyone taps. Getting back to an open position has to be fast for people who already trade here. A first install still has to reach one real action in that same session. Write down where a MetaMask person would already know what to tap, and where one of those apps does something we should study. Then write the roadmap, with the tradeoffs and the order, and what each step changes on Home. Own Home, the navigation and the defaults Own what a person sees when they open the app. That is balances, tokens and positions, and the way into Swap, Perps, Predict and Earn. A balance, a token, a position and an entry point are different, and Home has to show each one in a place the person can find. Decide what Home shows before anyone taps. Anything past that waits until the person opens it. Put Swap, Perps and Predict close together for people who use more than one of them. A person using only one of those products still has to be able to finish their action. Search and any assistance that sits on Home are part of this surface. You specify what stays on the screen, and what the person still confirms before money moves. Get people to a first action, and get them to come back You own the first session and the return after it. Count how many new wallets take a real action, and how many of those people open the app again. Define the path from the open to a confirmed action, and instrument it. Review screens with design You have a design background, and the role uses it. You review screens for hierarchy, type, spacing and what a tap does. You mark up a screen and say what should change, in writing, so design and engineering can build from the note. You write the product point of view clearly enough that design and engineering can build it. You are in the design reviews. A crowded screen, or a tap that does the wrong thing, gets the specific change you want. Work with the products that live on Home Each MetaMask trade primitive already has their own product manager. You own the entry from Home and the way their positions and balances read there. When a change to Home helps one product and hurts another, you name the tradeoff in the spec. You coordinate the sequencing when a Home change depends on one of those teams, or when one of their launches depends on Home. The spec says what Home will show on the day their change ships. Run the work You take a problem through spec, design, build, test and launch, and then you read what happened. You run planning and the backlog with the Core UX engineering team. A feature is done when the edge cases are handled, the screen is fast enough to use, and we can see the result in the data. That includes an empty Home, a person who uses one product, and a person who uses several. When something breaks, you write down what happened and what we will change in the screen or in the process, so the same break has a fix. Who You Are You have owned the main screen of a consumer product people open every day, and you stayed on it through launch. You can set a direction for that screen and stay with the tickets until it ships. You: Mark up a screen and say what the tap should do. Know which KPI a given screen is supposed to impact, and tell a real move from noise. Write a short spec that engineering and design can build, and use that same note when you walk a director through the decision. Sit with someone who already uses the app and someone who just installed, and turn what they do into the next change on Home. Work on a live product, where the screen is already open and the next change has to ship on top of it. Don't meet all the requirements? Don't sweat it.
